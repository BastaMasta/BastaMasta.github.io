whoami

Sameed Ahmed. I write software that runs close to the metal, and when it needs a body, I design the board it runs on.

Most of my time goes into Rust — a hobby kernel that boots and then usually panics, a CHIP‑8 interpreter, and command-line tools that strangers have actually installed. Before Rust I wrote enough 8086 assembly to permanently respect what compilers do for us.

I self-host nearly everything I use. Not on principle — I just like knowing where my data sleeps.

I also run ZapBurrito Studios, which is a game studio in roughly the same way a kernel that panics is an operating system: technically, and eventually.

The bench

Where software stops being an abstraction. Boards drawn in KiCad, assembled by hand, debugged with a multimeter and optimism.

Try the BinaryKeeb

The real one has two keys and lives on this bench. Below is the same idea in software, so you can feel it: tap eight bits, most significant first, and out comes the character they spell. Exactly as slow as it sounds — that's the point.

Two keys, 0 and 1. Enter eight bits and it emits the matching ASCII character. Interactive with JavaScript enabled.

How a board gets made here

Schematic in KiCad, routed by hand, sent to a fab — then the interesting part: finding out which of my assumptions the physical world disagrees with. The ground plane that looked optional. The trace that met spec on paper and picks up the regulator sitting next to it. Nothing on a bench is quite the part on the datasheet.

The boards themselves — Lumio V2, the BinaryKeeb, the TARS replica — are in Cartridges, with their sources.
